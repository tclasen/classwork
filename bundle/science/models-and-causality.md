# Models and explanations

A **model** is a purpose-specific representation that selects variables,
relationships, assumptions, and scope from a more complex subject. A model can
describe, predict, or explain; its usefulness is relative to the question and
does not make it identical to reality.

**Causation** is a relationship in which changing a condition is expected to
change an outcome under a stated model and context. An association or temporal
sequence may be evidence for a causal hypothesis but does not establish it by
itself. An **alternative explanation** is a competing model that could also
account for the observation. Controls, interventions, comparisons, and
independent evidence help distinguish alternatives.

[Causation and dependency](../foundations/causation-and-dependency.md) gives
the general distinction; [observation, measurement, and study design](observation-measurement-and-study-design.md)
shows how a design produces evidence.
