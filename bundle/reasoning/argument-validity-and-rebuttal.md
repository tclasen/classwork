# Premises and conclusions

An **argument** presents premises and a conclusion together with an implicit or
explicit warrant connecting them. An argument is **valid** when the conclusion
would follow if its premises and warrant held. It is **sound** when it is valid
and its premises are true or adequately established. An inductive argument can
increase support without guaranteeing its conclusion.

A **rebuttal** identifies a condition, counterexample, alternative explanation,
or missing premise that weakens the conclusion. Review therefore asks whether
the premises are relevant, sufficient, independent where needed, and applicable
to the stated scope. [Claims, evidence, and inference](../foundations/claims-evidence-and-inference.md)
supplies the basic vocabulary; an [assurance case](../assurance/assurance-case.md)
applies it to auditable system judgments.
