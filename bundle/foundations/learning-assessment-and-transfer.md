# Capability and demonstration

**Learning** is a change in what a learner knows, understands, can do, or is
disposed to do. A **capability** is what a learner can perform under specified
conditions. **Performance** is what is actually demonstrated on a task.
Performance is evidence about capability, but one observation may be too narrow
or poorly designed to establish it.

An **assessment** gathers and interprets evidence against stated criteria. A
mastery judgment should identify the task, context, quality threshold, and
uncertainty; “not yet demonstrated” is not identical to “does not know.”
**Transfer** is applying learning in a different but related context. Transfer
tests whether the learner can recognize and use the underlying structure rather
than merely repeat a memorized surface pattern.[^how-people-learn-ii]

This foundation explains the learning role of [competency and prerequisite structure](../education/competency-and-prerequisite-structure.md). Its
assessment evidence differs from [scientific evidence](claims-evidence-and-inference.md),
which supports claims about a subject rather than judgments about a learner.

[^how-people-learn-ii]: National Academies, [How People Learn II: Learners, Contexts, and Cultures](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/24783/chapter/2).
